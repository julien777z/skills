# Idea mode

The `research-games` skill's guidance for drafting, ranking and showing three game ideas from a study.

## Draft

Draft three distinct, original ideas as product facts holding every required field of `roblox-studio`'s game brief reference. Ground each pillar in a synthesis pattern and name its count with the study's qualifiers, and name the design bets the study does not cover. Borrow mechanics and conventions, never a studied game's name, characters, story, levels, art or look.

## Competing games

List each idea's competing games from the study's records: the `competitors` of the studied games in the idea's genre and subgenre, and those studied games themselves, keeping the ones a player of the idea would otherwise play, with each one's link, what it shares with the idea and how the idea differs. Look further on the charts only when the records hold no game of the idea's genre.

## Rank

Give the reasons for each place: how much of the study's evidence the idea rests on, how well it fits its audience, how crowded its competition is, the size and risk of its first release, and its design bets.

## Dashboard

An Overview page holds the ranking and why. One Proposals page holds a tab per idea, in ranked order. Each tab opens with a picture walkthrough: stacked `figures` of five or six original illustrations that follow a player through the game, such as its world map, the first minute, the core loop, a round or mode, progression or collection and a story beat, each with a one- or two-sentence description. Draw every idea's illustrations in one Roblox-like style, with low-poly blocky avatars and buildings, an angled map with name tags, and the on-screen interface as dark rounded panels, each idea keeping its own palette and mood. Below the walkthrough the tab holds the full write-up: every brief field, then its competing games and the evidence it rests on.
