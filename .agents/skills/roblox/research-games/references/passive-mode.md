# Passive mode

The `research-games` skill's procedure for a passive study: what each researcher reads for its games, from the experience page and popular gameplay videos, and what it records.

Open each game's experience page. Read its description, inspect its thumbnails, and determine the advertised core loop. Record its `genre` and subgenre from the games API, and its competitors: up to five games from the charts the study read, in the same subgenre and then the same genre, most-played first, each with what it shares with the game. Distinguish marketing claims, observed gameplay, and interpretation. Thumbnails and promotional art establish advertised style only, **never actual in-game UI, control behavior, tutorial flow, or font sizes**; those come from gameplay footage or play, and stay unknown without it.

**Study popular gameplay videos for every game.** They show skilled play through the main loop, rendered properly, without the runner. Start with the game's own preview video: the games API's media list names each `GamePreviewVideo` by `videoId`, and `https://assetdelivery.roblox.com/v1/asset/?id=<videoId>` serves it as WebM without YouTube; it is the creator's edited trailer and is recorded as such. Find them with `yt-dlp`, searching YouTube sorted by views (`https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=<game>+roblox&sp=CAM%253D`), once for the game and again with words such as `first time playing` and `beginner guide`. Read each candidate's upload date, channel, subscriber count and views, and pick up to three that show this game's ordinary play, never an animation, a skit, an exploit showcase or another game: one from a new player's first join, one of an experienced player's guide or tips, and one longer ordinary session. Among those, prefer high views, established channels and uploads from the last six months. Download only the stretches studied, at 480p and at most ten minutes each, with the auto-captions; extract frames every ten seconds and at scene changes with `ffmpeg`; confirm each frame shows this game before relying on it; and delete the video once its frames and captions are kept. Install `yt-dlp` and `ffmpeg` through the host's package manager when they are missing. Pace `yt-dlp` requests; a rate-limited request retries after pauses of one, three and five minutes, and when the restriction outlasts those, stop, name the missing picks in the records and return them `partial`. Infer player behaviour from the frame sequence and captions: what they do first, where they go, what they skip, and how often rewards and prompts appear, marking inference as inference. Record each video's URL, upload date, channel, subscribers, views and the timestamps relied on. Footage is edited, may show an older version and usually a returning player; record those limits, and do not call watching a video playtesting.

For each game, record concise notes on:

- First understandable action, repeated action/reward loop, and visible goals.
- Feedback for success: sound, particles, motion, reward UI, collection effects, and apparent cadence. Give timings only when measured from footage, from the video's own timestamps.
- Progression, mastery, variety, discovery, personalization, social/cooperative play, and useful companions/pets where actually evidenced.
- Friction or unclear onboarding visible from the material, and which attractive ideas fit another genre.
- Evidence URLs and uncertainty, including unavailable media. A description alone cannot establish control feel, retention, or the cause of popularity.
