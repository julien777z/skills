# Storyline

Create a story players want to act in, not a synopsis pasted over chores. Work across genres; obtain game-specific facts from the current project. Distinguish proposed canon from implemented and playtested content.

## Dependencies

- `subagent-matrix`
- `council`
- `roblox-gameplay`
- `share-media`

## Workflow

1. Establish mode: **create** for a new story, **audit** for an existing story, **extend** for a completed arc receiving new scope. Read the durable canon and actual first-launch flow, onboarding, quests, dialogue, character visuals, world layout, progression, ending, aftermath, and revisits. Follow the complete player journey before deciding what to preserve or revise; a late-game quest list or previous synopsis is insufficient. Record the audience, tone, player role, gameplay loop, scope, constraints, existing canon, and known implementation gaps. State reasonable assumptions; ask only for decisions that materially block the work.
2. Read the [story criteria](references/story-criteria.md) and [mode criteria](references/modes.md). Define what success means for this game before comparing proposals. Include character staging: where and why enemies and NPCs are present, what they are doing, when they become visible or interactive, and how the player meets them. In audit mode, identify concrete weak beats and contradictions with source pointers, then preserve what already works.
3. **Propose directions as short pitches first.** Invoke `subagent-matrix` at its standard capability level for **three independent narrative subagents**, each with the same brief and source reading list: the audience, tone, player role, gameplay loop, scope, constraints and existing canon from step 1, and the story criteria's **Concrete adventure** bar. Give each a different assigned starting angle — such as a different antagonist, kind of threat or first world — and nothing else that differs, so three readers of one audit do not converge on the same idea. Record every proposal worker's catalog state, exact dispatch identifier, assignment state and host receipt with its pitch. Each returns one **Direction pitch** as the [story criteria](references/story-criteria.md) define it, never a full proposal: no beats, dialogue, art or schema. Do not give them other pitches or prescribe the winning twist. Queue work if concurrency is limited. Their scope is story pitches, not concurrent edits to game code. When a required proposal worker cannot launch, apply `subagent-matrix`'s launch fallback for its candidate.
4. **Review the pitches for engagement and the main idea only, then put them to the user.** Up to three fresh reviewers at the standard level read the pitches for how engaging each is and what its main idea is, against the **Concrete adventure** bar; consistency, pacing, onboarding and budgets wait for approval. A pitch that fails the bar is revised once or dropped; if none passes, request a focused second round of at most two additional pitches. Put the pitches that pass to the user as plain pitches with a recommendation, each in its few lines and not in a file or dashboard, through the question tool as the global rules' **Asking the user** says, with each one's biggest risk stated honestly. The user's own pick approves a direction; "none of these" starts a fresh short round that uses what they said was wrong; an agent's own preference never approves one. **Nothing below runs before that approval.**
5. **Develop the approved direction, then attack it before polishing.** One worker develops the pitch into the structured proposal below, with its frame and next two arcs and the measurable budgets from the [story criteria](references/story-criteria.md): beat count, total player-facing words, per-line and per-conversation limits, the progression range and unlock cadence, and, where the story has an antagonist, its on-screen lines. Then invoke `subagent-matrix` at its standard capability level for one fresh critic subagent, recording its catalog state, exact dispatch identifier, assignment state and host receipt with its findings and applying the launch fallback when it cannot launch. Give it only the developed story and the story criteria, never the pitches or the selection rationale. It plays the story twice — as the audience's youngest player who reads only each conversation's first line, and as a player who reads everything — and runs the criteria's **Critique checks**. It returns every failing beat and line with a concrete rewrite. Apply each confirmed finding to the story; a finding you would reject goes to `council` in **Ask** mode and its ruling is followed; a finding that changes a premise or the climax gets one further critic round on the revised story. Record findings and their dispositions with the selection.
6. Polish a complete story web and implementation map. Expand regions, routes, worlds, or maps when they give the story distinct playable beats. Propose major characters’ appearance and motion and compare the cast together: identify how each is recognizable without a nameplate, including non-color cues, and specify variants where shared base assets make different characters look alike. Existing rigs are a baseline to evaluate, not an appearance proposal. Then identify required environment, art, systems, dialogue, and progression changes, separate essential scope from optional scope, and preserve explicit user limits. A scope proposal is not proof that a map exists. Separate major worlds into the project's established place/level boundaries.
7. In create/audit/extend design-only requests, return the proposed canon and implementation plan. When implementation is authorized, apply the selected improvements through the game's existing content owners and applicable implementation skills. Keep rewards, quest gates, UI instructions, destinations, environmental states, and recap text consistent. Verify the actual first launch, onboarding, playable sequence, ending, and revisits, including character arrivals, activities, interactions, and departures from the player's normal approach. Record proposed, implemented, and playtested status per beat or feature with evidence; prose and generated art do not prove playable implementation. Update durable canon with appearances, staging, relationships, learned concepts, setups/payoffs, and preserved victories so future audits can read the full story.

## Output

A pitch is plain text in the pitch's fields. Once a direction is approved, each subagent and the orchestrator returns valid YAML using the [output schema](references/output-schema.yaml). Populate every section; use empty lists and explicit `unknown` values rather than inventing evidence. IDs are stable within the artifact, and character relationships, beat prerequisites, story-web edges, and setup/payoff links reference existing story IDs. A proposal may leave `selection` empty; the final artifact includes selection rationale, rejected alternatives, critique findings and their dispositions, concrete changes, unresolved risks, and verification status. Return a concise player-facing premise, and send the full YAML as `share-media`'s **A file the user should see reaches them where they read** bullet requires. Keep spoilers in the artifact rather than accidentally revealing them in player-facing copy.

## Guardrails

- Every arc resolves its central dramatic question and earns its emotional ending. Future hooks do not excuse an unfinished current story, and each arc keeps the frame open as the criteria's **Room to continue** says.
- Extend consequences of previous victories; do not erase them, resurrect a defeated threat without groundwork, or retroactively make the player's actions pointless. Mark any proposed retcon and explain its necessity.
- Story length comes from meaningful developments, relationships, discoveries, and varied play—not repeated errands, long exposition, arbitrary travel, or word count.
- Audit candidly. Preserve names and mechanics when they work; recommend substantial changes when evidence supports them, with what each requires and its continuity consequences.
