# Mode criteria

## Create

A complete new arc has original characters, a dramatic premise, the player's role, a sequence of meaningful developments, a final confrontation or decision, and an aftermath. Existing mechanics constrain how it becomes playable. Unknown audience or production budgets are stated assumptions.

## Audit

The full implemented journey is the baseline, including first launch and onboarding. Read the canon’s appearances, learned concepts, relationships, unresolved setups, fulfilled payoffs, and preserved victories, then compare them with current implementation. Findings separate observed defects, inferred player-experience risks, and taste preferences. Each finding names evidence, player impact, a proposed repair, and affected consumers. Keeping a strong beat is a valid decision; preserving a weak beat because it already exists is not. A rewritten ending must still answer the original arc's questions, unless an explicit, justified premise change replaces them consistently.

## Extend

The complete earlier journey, from onboarding through revisits, is input canon, including fulfilled promises, irreversible choices, relationships, and the ending players already experienced. New scope follows from that aftermath. A coda or changed final invitation may connect the arcs while preserving earned resolution. Retcons are explicit proposed changes, never silently revised history. New worlds have their own complete arcs, connective characters/themes, accessible revisits, and a final state even when later adventures remain possible.
