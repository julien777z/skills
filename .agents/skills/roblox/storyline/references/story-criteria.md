# Story quality criteria

A strong story has a clear initial desire, a disruption the player understands, escalating consequences, a midpoint that changes interpretation, a costly or revealing choice, and a resolved climax followed by a visible aftermath. The player causes the decisive outcome through the game's verbs. Character motives exist before the player arrives and change under pressure.

Characters have distinct voices, wants, needs, fears, limitations, secrets, relationships, and reasons to help or oppose one another. Opposition has understandable motives without requiring moral equivalence. Reveals have observable setup and later payoff; a twist must explain prior evidence rather than contradict it. NPCs contribute actions and consequences, not only exposition. Dialogue is concise enough to play, with deeper optional material where appropriate.

Each world has a local dramatic question, visual identity, inhabitants, playable activities, arrival/departure logic, and a satisfying local resolution. Regions connect geographically and causally. Expansion earns its size; repeated tasks change context, stakes, strategy, or relationships. Safe return visits acknowledge completed events and cannot accidentally reset or re-award progression.

The story web includes causal, relationship, knowledge, location, prerequisite, and setup/payoff edges. Dependencies have no impossible cycles; optional content is never required knowledge for a mandatory reveal. Co-op players and returning players can understand their own current objective. Tutorial beats teach while advancing character or conflict, and crucial information survives skipped optional dialogue through readable objective/recap text.

Score each candidate from 1 (absent/contradictory) to 5 (specific and convincing) on coherence, character agency, playable variety, emotional payoff, onboarding clarity, and feasibility. A candidate with an unresolved central ending, contradictory mandatory dependencies, or a passive player climax fails regardless of total score. Scores compare design judgment; they are not measured retention predictions.
